# ADR-188: sdr-pluto-rx, the owner of the PlutoSDR

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-10-26
**Cog:** `sdr-pluto-rx` 0.1.0

## Context

A PlutoSDR (Zynq-7010 with an AD9363A, 40 MHz reference) is attached to the Ventuno Q. Bench results (WeftOS `docs/plans/pluto-sdr-cogs-plan.md`, sections 2 to 2.2): USB 2.0 caps the sample stream at about 7.5 MS/s (4.3 to 4.7 over `usb:` and 5.5 to 5.9 over `ip:` on the Ventuno, which makes 5 MS/s the safe continuous rate); the firmware can run the chip as an AD9364 for 70 to 6000 MHz (rated 325 to 3800); a client that crashes can leave the chip in digital loopback, after which the receive path reads zeros; and `iio_readdev` does not report samples lost to the USB limit. Several consumers want the radio (a spectrum cog, ADS-B, ISM decoders) and there is one tuner.

## Decision

1. **One owner.** The cog is the only process that opens the radio. Consumers read the stream and call the service on the node's cogbus (WeftOS ADR-122). The device allowlist (`0456:b673`) is the P3 owner model's job.
2. **libiio through `dlopen`.** `src/iio.rs` binds about twenty functions of the libiio 0.x API by hand and loads the library when the radio is opened. No headers, no bindgen, no link-time dependency; a host without libiio still builds and runs `--simulate` and `--replay`. The `industrial-io` crate (MIT) was checked and not used: it needs the libiio headers and bindgen at build time, which the Ventuno image and the cross-build containers do not have. libiio is LGPL-2.1 or later and is loaded, not linked or copied. The 1.0 API is not bound; a missing symbol is reported by name.
3. **Known state on taking the radio.** Digital loopback off, the four DDS tones off (scale and raw), TX attenuation at its maximum (-89.75 dB). What was found and what was set are in `safe_state` of the status. The cog never enables a transmit buffer and `tx_config` is refused (the transmit cog is phase R5 of the plan).
4. **Backend measured at start.** `--backend auto` opens `ip:192.168.2.1` and `usb:` in turn (`ip:` first on Linux, `usb:` first on macOS), runs the receive path at 10 MS/s for 1.5 s and uses the faster, preferring the OS default unless the other is more than 5 percent faster. `--uri` overrides. The choice and the numbers are in `backend`.
5. **Reads are large, records are small.** The network backend cannot keep up with 5 MS/s reading 8192 samples at a time (3.2 MS/s measured, 30 percent loss); 32768 to 524288 samples were gap-free. The cog reads 65536 and cuts each read into 8192-sample ring records.
6. **The `iq.cs16.v1` stream** is a cogbus stream ring (class `stream`). A record is a 40-byte little-endian header and complex 16-bit samples: kind (samples or gap event), flags (discontinuity, band changed, all zero), segment (one per dwell), centre, rate, bandwidth, the sample index on the segment's clock (lost samples are counted in it), the samples lost just before the record, and the sample count. The ring's `seq` counts records; `t_ns` is the host monotonic clock at the end of the record's capture; `w_ns` is the wall clock at the write. A reader of the ring sees `seq` jumps (it fell behind) and gap events (the link lost samples) as different things. IQ is local to the node.
7. **Loss is counted from the clock** (`src/loss.rs`). The stream has no hardware timestamps and an overflow is silent, so each read's arrival is compared with `rate x elapsed`. The deficit is measured against a baseline (the smallest deficit in the last 5 to 10 s, which absorbs crystal drift of tens of ppm) and counts as lost only when the excess stays above the slack (20 ms of data, or four reads) for 250 ms; the smallest excess in that time is what is counted. Resolution, stated in the guide: a loss under about 20 ms, or a loss rate under about 0.1 percent, is not told apart from jitter and drift; a gap is declared about 250 ms after it happened, so the sample clock jumps then, not at the missing samples. `samples.expected` against `samples.delivered` is the raw comparison; `pending_unconfirmed` shows a loss in progress. Checked: at 5 MS/s gap-free over an hour (tests with a 30 ppm clock error and with 4 ms jitter count zero), at 7.5 and 10 MS/s the lost count tracks `expected - delivered` to within the pending part.
8. **Band plans and one tuner.** A consumer's `tune` is a one-band plan; `plan` gives up to 16 bands (centre, rate, bandwidth, gain, dwell). All plans run round-robin, one dwell each, with the time each plan got reported as `duty`. The plan from the command line runs only while no consumer has its own. A `lease` is exclusive for up to an hour: the round-robin pauses and other callers' tuning is refused with the holder's name. A retune destroys and recreates the capture buffer so no sample of the old tuning follows, then discards the first read; segments start at every retune.
9. **Low rates.** Below 2.083 MS/s the cog loads a 64-tap decimating FIR (2 or 4) with the ad9361 driver's file format, enables it, then sets the rate, which reaches 521 kS/s; above it the FIR is switched off again. Checked on the board.
10. **Range.** Bands are accepted from 70 to 6000 MHz, rates 0.52 to 61.44 MS/s, bandwidth 0.2 to 56 MHz. Outside the rated 325 to 3800 MHz a band is marked `uncalibrated` in the status and in `tune` replies.
11. **Status.** `sdr.status/<source>` (`sdr.status.v1`, `telemetry.v1`-style items) every 2 s: LO, rate, bandwidth, gain, RSSI, PHY and Zynq temperatures, power, delivered, expected, lost, pending, loss fraction, gap events, overflows, zero blocks, dead receive path, retunes. `/healthz` fails when no read arrived for 5 s or four consecutive reads were all zero. A radio that vanishes (any `ENODEV`, `EIO`, `ETIMEDOUT`, `EPIPE` class error) is counted as a disconnect and reopened with backoff; nothing is power-cycled. Seen on the overnight soak: the Pluto dropped off the bus twice (`usb 1-2: device descriptor read/64, error -110`, reset, disconnect, re-enumerated 40 to 60 s later, about 80 s each from `Broken pipe` to streaming again); the cog reported `no_source` with the reason, retried with backoff, and streamed again each time with `disconnects` at 2. A temperature outside -40 to 125 degrees C (the AD9361's sensor read -80 and -75 after the second return) is dropped, not published.
12. **Service.** `svc.sdr/<source>` (`sdr.v1`): `tune`, `set_rate`, `set_bw`, `set_gain`, `plan`, `plan_remove`, `lease`, `release`, `status`, `tx_config` (refused). The caller is the broker's `from`; plans belong to their caller.
13. **Contract.** stdout JSON per interval and an 8-float store vector (id 29: centre GHz, rate MS/s, power dBFS, loss fraction, PHY temperature, Zynq temperature, zero-block fraction, gap events), `/status /raw /guide /healthz` on `127.0.0.1:8063`, `--once --window N`, `--simulate`, `--record FILE` and `--replay FILE` (a `PLUTOIQ1` capture: a 32-byte header and whole reads of interleaved i16).
13b. **Hub sharing.** `usb_hub` in the status lists the other devices on the Pluto's USB root hub from sysfs (`/sys/bus/usb/devices`, found by 0456:b673), whether any streams isochronously now (a camera or microphone alternate setting with an isochronous endpoint), and `advised_max_rate_sps`: 5 MS/s alone, 4 MS/s with an isochronous device streaming (measured 5.00 and 4.33 MS/s with a 720p MJPEG camera). `rate_over_advice` is true while the tuned rate exceeds it. The cog reports and advises; it does not lower the rate itself, and moving a device to another hub is the owner's decision.
14. **Broker keep-alive.** The cog answers the broker's `{"type":"ping"}` with `{"op":"pong","id":0}` (cogbus gap 4); a refused `stream_open` (`busy`) is retried every 2 s.

## Consequences

- The schemas `iq.cs16.v1`, `sdr.status.v1` and `sdr.v1` are described here and in the guide; WeftOS must register them in its contracts before the broker's manifest check can name them in a node deployment.
- Stalls longer than the kernel's buffer depth lose samples whatever the cog does; the cog reports them and does not try to recover them.
- `sdr.status` is published by the acquisition thread between reads, so a retune that takes a second delays it by a second.
- A second reader of the ring that wants no loss must use the `block` policy and accept the writer waiting up to the deadline; the default `drop-oldest` readers see ring `seq` jumps when they fall behind.
- Heat and power remain the operator's: the Zynq idles near 60 degrees C; both temperatures are in the status.

## Verification

Unit tests cover the loss counter (steady arrival, jitter, an hour of 30 ppm drift, a stall that recovers, a lost stretch, a rate-limited stream, the sample clock), the scheduler (round-robin, duty, plan ownership, leases, the fallback plan), the call parser and its refusals (including `tx_config`), the ring and the bus client against a stand-in broker (descriptor passing included), the acquisition loop with the simulator and scripted radios (contiguous records, band switches, a tune call, a dead receive path, a vanished radio, a stall that never arrives), the HTTP endpoints, the FIR text and the capture file. `tests/cli.rs` runs the binary: `--help`, `--once --simulate`, `--record` then `--replay` giving the same samples, and the bus loop against a stand-in broker. The on-board run, the one-hour 2.4 GHz capture and the multi-hour band-plan soak are recorded in WeftOS `docs/plans/pluto-sdr-cogs-plan.md` section 2.3.
